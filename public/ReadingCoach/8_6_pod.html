英文原文閱讀 (English Text)

Borobudur is one of the most remarkable Buddhist monuments in the world. This temple is made from volcanic rock, and contains 504 statues of Buddha and thousands of other sculptures that share the Buddha’s teachings, life, and wisdom. Borobudur is located in the Kedu valley of central Java, a large Indonesian island. It lies between two volcanoes and two rivers. The ancient Javanese considered the Kedu Valley to be one of the most sacred sites in Java because of its fertile land and high agricultural production.

Historians believe that Borobudur was completed around the year 800 CE. Several centuries earlier, Hindu and Buddhist merchants from India had settled in the region. The Javanese incorporated parts of the culture and religions of ancient India into their own. Borobudur’s architecture is an example of this blending of cultures because it contains both ancient Indian and Javanese influences. Buddhists among the Javanese population made pilgrimages and performed rituals at this holy site up until the 15th century.

After the 15th century, the temple was abandoned. Some historians believe Borobudur was abandoned because many Javanese converted to Islam during this time. Others argue that volcanic eruptions near Borobudur may have stopped people from visiting this site. However, much mystery remains about why this site was abandoned. As time went by, the temple was covered in volcanic ash and vegetation. It was no longer visible. Although local people knew of this temple from legends, the site was no longer used.

In 1814, an English lieutenant governor heard these legends from the islanders. He traveled to the place the local people had talked about and uncovered the temple from under layers of volcanic ash. Archaeologists worked with the Indonesian government to restore the temple from 1907 to 1911 and again in the 1970s. Although the full history of this temple remains unclear, historians have uncovered certain facts about the structure’s symbolism.

The structure of Borobudur symbolizes the Buddhist path to enlightenment. Borobudur is a stepped pyramid that has three main levels. These main levels represent the three spheres of the universe, according to Buddhist understanding. They symbolize the spiritual journey through which individuals try to achieve “formlessness,” or full detachment from the physical world and earthly desires.

The temple’s first level is a square base that represents kamadhatu. This is the Sphere of Desire, or the lowest sphere of the Buddhist universe. On this first level, there are sculptures of earthly desires and pleasures. The second level represents rupadhatu, the Sphere of Forms. It consists of five square terraces. On this level, sculptures of events from the Buddha’s life line the passageways. The highest level contains three circular terraces. It represents arupadhatu, the Sphere of Formlessness. This is the highest sphere of existence in Buddhist ideology and the last step on the path to enlightenment. On the highest level of the temple, there are dome-shaped shrines to the Buddha called stupas. These stupas symbolize the enlightened mind, a mind free of earthly desires and attachments.

The temple’s three levels are connected by open-air passageways. The passageways circle around a central point. Buddhists who visit this site travel clockwise through these passageways until they reach the top level. In Buddhist philosophy, individuals work towards enlightenment by developing concentration, morality, and wisdom. Over the centuries, walking the paths of Borobudur has allowed Buddhists to meditate on their spiritual journey. Historians have also learned about the history of the Javanese people through this architectural marvel.
