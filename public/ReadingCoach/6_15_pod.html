亞馬遜雨林 - 英文語感探索

Podcast 節目逐字稿

The Amazon Rainforest is the largest tropical rainforest in the world. It covers about 2.1 million square miles across nine countries in South America. Most of the forest is in Brazil, which contains about 60 percent of the Amazon. The forest is named after the Amazon River, which flows through it.

The Amazon Rainforest is incredibly biodiverse. Scientists estimate that it is home to about 10 percent of all the species on Earth. There are approximately 40,000 plant species, 1,300 bird species, and 3,000 types of fish in the Amazon. New species are discovered there every year. The forest is also home to many indigenous peoples who have lived there for thousands of years.

The Amazon Rainforest plays a critical role in regulating the world's climate. The trees absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and release oxygen. This is why the Amazon is often called the "lungs of the Earth." The forest also helps regulate rainfall patterns across South America.

However, the Amazon Rainforest faces serious threats. Deforestation is the biggest problem. Farmers and ranchers clear large areas of forest for agriculture and cattle grazing. Logging companies cut down trees for timber. Mining operations also destroy parts of the forest. Between 2001 and 2020, the Amazon lost about 10 percent of its tree cover.

Many organizations are working to protect the Amazon. Some groups help indigenous communities defend their land rights. Others work to create protected areas where no development is allowed. Scientists study the forest to better understand its ecosystems and find ways to preserve them. Saving the Amazon is important for the entire planet.

主持人
