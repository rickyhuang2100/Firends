What happens when a volcano erupts? A lot of people first think of the molten rock, or lava, that explodes or oozes out of a volcano during an eruption. But that's not all that happens. When a volcano erupts, it impacts the atmosphere around it. Volcanic eruptions can affect the weather, temperature, and air quality. When a volcano erupts, it might release particles of ash. It might also release gases like sulfur dioxide, carbon dioxide, and water vapor. The particles and gases can cause vog, or volcanic smog, which makes the air quality poor. And these substances all impact the weather near the volcano. They can even have lasting impacts on the rest of the planet. During some volcano eruptions, large amounts of ash and gases are released into the atmosphere. The ash and gases can block sunlight and temporarily cool the area near the volcano. Some large ash particles fall out of the air quickly. These large particles do not impact the temperature for very long, as they fall out of the atmosphere within a few days. However, gases and smaller ash particles form dark clouds that block out a lot of the sun's rays. And gases together with even smaller particles of ash can stay in the atmosphere for months! These tiny ash particles are light enough to be carried over long distances. The gases and tiny particles might even block sunlight and cause cooling over large areas of the earth. One of the gases that might be emitted by a volcano is sulfur dioxide. Sulfur dioxide combines with water in the atmosphere. This combination is sulfuric acid. Like the ash particles and gases, these droplets of sulfuric acid also reflect the incoming rays of the sun. The earth's surface and atmosphere below the droplets cool when the sun's rays cannot reach these levels. Sulfuric acid droplets can stay in the atmosphere for three years and travel around the world. The droplets can also fall to the earth's surface as acid rain near the volcano eruption site. This happens when the droplets combine with ash particles in the air and grow large enough to fall. Acid rain can raise the acidity of lakes, soils, and seas. This rise in acidity can damage plants, harm animals, and contaminate drinking water. Volcanic eruptions might also release large amounts of carbon dioxide and water vapor into the atmosphere. Carbon dioxide and water vapor are greenhouse gases. Carbon dioxide traps heat in the atmosphere and causes the planet to warm. Usually, volcanoes do not release enough carbon dioxide to affect the global climate. But there have been moments in history where a series of volcanic eruptions has significantly increased the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. Volcanic eruptions can have a powerful short-term impact on the lives of people living close to an eruption site. But the global, far-reaching, and sometimes long-term impacts of volcanic eruptions are also incredibly powerful.
