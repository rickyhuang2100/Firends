英文原文 Text

Architecture, like painting, literature, and other forms of art, reflects the ideals of the people who build it. The Parthenon is one of the best examples of ancient Greek architecture. The structure, created between 447 B.C.E. and 432 B.C.E., tells us a lot about the ancient Greek way of thinking.

In ancient Greece, balance and order were important principles. The ancient Greeks believed that everything around them happened for a reason. They wanted to find out this reason and discover the order of the world around them. Their buildings were beautiful, but they do not overflow with frivolity or emotion. Instead, ancient Greek buildings define order.

Columns are one hallmark of classical structure that we associate with Greece and Rome. The Parthenon was built with eight columns on the front and seventeen on each side. The building is symmetrical and balanced. Mathematical principles make the design stable. Almost the entire structure is made of marble.

The Parthenon was located in ancient Athens. This was a polis, or "city-state," of ancient Greece. Another well-known city-state was Sparta. Each city-state of ancient Greece had its own government. In fact, the balance and order that we see in the Parthenon echo the principles of the ancient Athens government. Ancient Athens was ruled by the people in a democracy. Citizens debated the actions to be taken by the government. This was very different from a monarchy where kings, queens, or emperors ruled the people. The citizens within ancient Athens ran their own government. This system of government would require balance—no one person could become too powerful. It would also require order so that the government would not crumble.

Today, parts of the Parthenon continue to stand. Have you ever visited it?
