AI Podcast 逐字稿：伊斯蘭藝術的抽象與幾何之美

Original English Text

The typical depiction of nature in Islamic art is abstract. This approach departs from Europe’s naturalistic approach, which traditionally aimed to portray nature in a realistic way. Plants and flowers looked exactly like real plants and flowers in European art, while in Islamic art, these natural objects would often be more geometric and linear. Islamic art is known for being geometric, so geometric vegetal art fit into the prominent aesthetic. Vegetal patterns can be found everywhere in Islamic art and often serve an ornamental function. One of the reasons for their popular usage is that some sects of Islam forbid artists from representing the natural world in any of its living forms. Consequently, artists were not allowed to represent plants or flowers in their artwork. Another reason can be attributed to their origin. Art historians believe that the rulers of the early Islamic empire encouraged artists to focus on creating impressive quantities of high-scale objects. As a result, artists did not focus their energy on inventing new designs, and instead relied on the artistic practices of Rome, Egypt, and the Byzantine empire. Over time, however, Islamic artists departed from these established artistic traditions and Islamic vegetal patterns became highly stylized and geometric.
