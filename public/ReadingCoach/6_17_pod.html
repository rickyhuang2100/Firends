奧運歷史與英文學習 Podcast 深度解析

原始英文文本 (English Text)

The Olympic Games are the world's greatest athletic competition. They bring together athletes from nearly every country on Earth. But the Olympics have a history that goes back thousands of years.

The ancient Olympic Games began in 776 B.C.E. in Olympia, Greece. At first, the only event was a short foot race called the stadion. Over time, more events were added, including wrestling, boxing, chariot racing, and the pentathlon. Only men were allowed to compete, and they competed without any clothing. The ancient Games were held every four years for over 1,000 years until they were banned in 393 C.E. by the Roman Emperor Theodosius.

The modern Olympic Games were revived in 1896 by a Frenchman named Pierre de Coubertin. The first modern Olympics were held in Athens, Greece, in honor of the ancient Games. About 280 athletes from 13 countries participated. All of them were men.

Women first competed in the Olympics in 1900 at the Paris Games. Since then, women's participation has grown steadily. At the 2020 Tokyo Olympics, women made up nearly 49 percent of all athletes.

The Winter Olympics were first held in 1924 in Chamonix, France. They include sports like skiing, ice skating, and ice hockey. Until 1992, the Winter and Summer Olympics were held in the same year. Now they alternate every two years.

Today, the Olympics include over 300 events in more than 30 sports. The Games continue to evolve, with new sports being added regularly. The Olympic motto is "Faster, Higher, Stronger — Together," reflecting the spirit of athletic excellence and international cooperation.
