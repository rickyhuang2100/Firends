雙語 Podcast 深度解析逐字稿 (輕鬆閱讀版)

Original Text

Chocolate has a long and fascinating history. The story of chocolate begins in Central and South America, where the cacao tree grows. The ancient Maya people were among the first to use cacao beans. They ground the beans into a paste and mixed it with water, chili peppers, and other spices to create a bitter drink. The Maya believed that cacao was a gift from the gods.

The Aztecs also valued cacao highly. In fact, they used cacao beans as money. A rabbit could be bought for about ten cacao beans. The Aztec emperor Montezuma reportedly drank 50 cups of a chocolate drink every day.

When Spanish explorers arrived in the Americas in the 1500s, they brought cacao beans back to Europe. The Spanish added sugar to the bitter chocolate drink, making it much more appealing to European tastes. Chocolate drinks became very popular among the wealthy in Europe.

In the 1800s, chocolate changed forever. In 1828, a Dutch chemist invented a way to make powdered chocolate. This made chocolate cheaper and easier to produce. In 1847, a British company created the first chocolate bar. And in 1875, a Swiss man named Daniel Peter figured out how to add milk to chocolate, creating milk chocolate.

Today, chocolate is one of the most popular treats in the world. The global chocolate industry is worth over 100 billion dollars. Most of the world's cacao is grown in West Africa, particularly in Ivory Coast and Ghana. From a bitter drink of the ancient Maya to the sweet treats we enjoy today, chocolate has come a long way.
